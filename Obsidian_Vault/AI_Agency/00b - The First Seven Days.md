# The First Seven Days

The vault is organized by topic. This is organized by day. If you do not know what to do on Tuesday, do what Tuesday says.

The goal of week one is not a client. It is a working agent you can call and the first outreach sent. Everything after that is repetition.

Budget two to three hours a day. If you only have one, the plan still works, it just takes ten days.

## Day 1: Decide And Buy

Pick your niche and commit to it for at least ninety days. Do not agonize. The tiebreaker is access: if you know someone in an industry, start there. See [[01c - Pick Your Niche]] and the scored list in [[12a - How To Read This Directory]].

Then, in one sitting:

- Create your platform account. GoHighLevel entry plan is the worked example in this vault, see [[02b - GoHighLevel Account Setup]]. If you would rather not pay a monthly fee before you have clients, build on [[11g - The Budget Stack]] instead.
- Create one sub account for a fictional business in your niche. Give it a real sounding name and address.
- Buy a phone number in that sub account. LC Phone has to be enabled at the agency level first (Agency Settings > Phone Integration) or the page will not sell you one. Do not start A2P registration for the fictional business; it cannot pass. The demo runs on voice, and the two honest options for texting are in [[02h - A2P Registration Field By Field]].
- Buy a domain and set up a branded email on it, like you@yourdomain.com. See [[02f - Your Own Infrastructure]].

Do not buy anything else. Not a website, not a logo, not another tool. See [[02a - The Minimum Stack]].

**Done when:** you have an account, a sub account, a phone number, and a branded email.

## Day 2: Build The Calendar And The Skeleton

Calendar first, because the agent is built on top of it.

- Create a calendar with realistic appointment types and durations
- Set minimum notice, buffers, and availability windows
- Book a test appointment manually and confirm it appears where you expect. See [[02e - Calendars And Booking]].

Then create the agent with only a greeting and a two line prompt. Call it. It should answer and speak. The two lines:

```
You are Ava, the receptionist for Riverside Family Dental. Your job is to book the caller for an appointment.
Keep every response to one or two sentences and ask one question at a time.
```

Screens and field names are in [[03h - GoHighLevel Voice AI Screen By Screen]].

Do not add anything else until that works. See [[03b - Creating Your First Agent]].

**Done when:** you called a phone number and an AI answered.

## Day 3: Write The Real Prompt

Open [[09a - Master Agent Prompt Template]] and fill it in for your fictional business.

Invent the business facts. Make up five common questions and their answers, the way a real business in your niche would have them. Keep the hard rules section intact.

Connect the calendar to the agent. Then call and try to book something. It will fail in some way. Fix it, call again.

If you have time left, build the confirmation and reminder workflows from [[02j - Confirmations And Reminders Built]]. The texts will not deliver until A2P is approved, and it will not be for a fictional business, so test the voice booking first and check the workflow's execution log rather than your phone.

Expect this day to take longer than you planned. See [[03c - Writing The Agent Prompt]].

**Done when:** you called the agent and it booked an appointment that actually appeared on the calendar.

## Day 4: Break It, Then Fix It

Run the full checklist in [[09g - Pre Launch Checklist]].

Every case will surface something. Interrupt it. Mumble. Demand a price. Ask for a human. Call from a noisy room. Have someone else call it.

Each failure becomes a line in your prompt. This day is where your agent stops being a demo and starts being a product. See [[03f - Testing Before You Ship]].

**Done when:** you can run the checklist with no serious failures, and someone else booked an appointment successfully.

## Day 5: Record And List

Two jobs.

**Record the demo.** Call the agent and book an appointment cleanly. Record it. This forty second file is now your entire sales process. See [[04b - The Demo First Method]]. Downloading, trimming, naming, and hosting the file is in [[03i - Recording And Sending The Demo Call]].

**Build the list.** Open a map, search your niche, and collect fifty businesses. Name, phone, website, owner name if you can find it.

Then call ten of them, after hours. Note what happened. That column is worth more than everything else in the sheet, because it turns a guess into a measurement. See [[04a - Building Your Target List]].

**Done when:** you have a recording and fifty names, ten of which you have personally called.

## Day 6: Send

Send outreach to twenty businesses using [[09c - Outreach Swipe File]].

For your five best prospects, spend an hour each building a custom demo with their actual business name, then send that recording. For the other fifteen, send the generic demo.

The fast way to build those five: save your demo sub account as a snapshot (agency view > Account Snapshots > Create), then create a new sub account per prospect with that snapshot loaded, change the business name and facts in the prompt, buy a number, and route it to the agent. Each demo costs one number rental, roughly a dollar or two a month (VERIFY), plus the test minutes. When a prospect says no or goes quiet for a month, release the number (Settings > Phone Numbers > release) and delete the sub account so you are not paying for ghosts. Keep the snapshot. See [[02d - Sub Accounts And Snapshots]].

Also write down everyone you personally know who owns a business or works at one. Message three of them today. Your first client is usually one of these people, not someone cold. See [[04c - Outreach That Gets Replies]].

**Done when:** twenty messages sent and three warm contacts messaged.

## Day 7: Set The Machine

Two hours of setup that makes the next eight weeks work.

- Print [[09d - Sales Call Script]] and read it out loud twice.
- Read [[04e - Handling Objections]] and say the responses out loud until they sound like you.
- Set your daily floor. Ten outreach touches, follow ups due, that is it. Small enough to hit on your worst day. See [[07b - Consistency Is The Strategy]].
- Put your follow up dates in a calendar. Four to five touches over three weeks.
- Pick where prospects are tracked and use only that. Either a pipeline in your own agency sub account (Opportunities > Pipelines, stages: Listed, Demo Sent, Replied, Call Booked, Proposal, Won, Lost) or a plain sheet with the same columns. The pipeline lets follow up workflows fire; the sheet is faster on day one. Both work. Two half kept systems do not.
- Write down the milestone list from [[08b - The Milestones Worth Tracking]] and check off what you have already done.

Then read [[07a - The Quit Point]]. Not because you need it today. Because you will need it in about two weeks and you want to have already seen it coming.

**Done when:** you know exactly what you are doing every day next week without deciding.

## Week Two And After

There is no day 8 through 60 plan because there is only one instruction: hit the floor every day.

Ten touches. Follow ups. Build a custom demo when a prospect is worth it. Take calls when they come.

That is the whole business until the first client. See [[07d - Score The Inputs]] for how to tell whether you are actually doing it or only feeling like you are.

## If You Fall Behind

You will. Something takes three days instead of one, usually the prompt.

Do not restart and do not skip ahead to outreach without a working agent. The demo is the product. A week of building it is not a delay, it is the thing that makes everything after it possible. See [[01d - Build One Before You Sell One]].
