# The Offer That Sells

The offer that fails is "AI automation for your business."

It fails because it is a category, not an outcome. The owner hears it and has no idea what changes on Monday. Vague offers get vague responses, which is how you end up with twenty conversations and zero clients.

The offer that works names one outcome, for one type of business, at one price.

"I set up an AI receptionist for dental practices that answers every call 24/7 and books appointments straight into your calendar. $1,500 to build it, $500 a month to run and maintain it."

Read that again and notice what it does. It says who it is for. It says exactly what happens. It says what it costs. There is nothing left to imagine.

## One Offer, Not A Menu

You will be tempted to offer everything: voice agents, chatbots, missed call text back, review automation, email campaigns, CRM setup. Do not.

A menu forces the buyer to make decisions. Decisions create friction, friction creates delay, delay kills deals. One offer means the only decision is yes or no.

You can upsell later, and you should. But the thing you lead with is one sentence.

## The Structure

Every offer that converts in this business has the same three parts.

**A setup fee.** Charged before you build anything. This filters out tire kickers and covers your time during the build. Anywhere from $500 to several thousand depending on your niche and proof.

**A monthly retainer.** This is the actual business. One time fees make you a freelancer. Recurring revenue makes you an agency worth something.

**A usage cap or passthrough.** Voice AI costs you per minute. Your pricing must account for the client who gets 400 calls a month. A simple illustrative version: 500 minutes included, $0.40 per minute after. See [[06b - Protecting Your Margin]].

## Name The Outcome In Their Numbers

The strongest version of your offer does the math for the buyer.

Take the vault's standard worked example, illustrative only: a new dental patient is worth about $1,500 and your retainer is $500 a month, or $1,500 a quarter. One recovered patient a quarter more than covers it. Anything past that is profit for the practice. Say that out loud on the call. Do not make them compute it. See [[04d - The Sales Call]].

If you cannot express the payback in one sentence, you picked the wrong niche. Go back to [[01c - Pick Your Niche]].
