# Pick Your Niche

Pick one type of business and go deep. Not "local businesses." One vertical.

The reason is leverage. When every client is a dental practice, your second build takes a fraction of the time of your first, because the objections are the same, the calendar setup is the same, and your agent prompt is 90% reusable. See [[02d - Sub Accounts And Snapshots]].

Generalists rebuild from scratch every time and stay slow forever.

## The Three Tests

Run any niche you are considering through these before you commit.

**The inbound call test.** Does this business get meaningful inbound phone calls from customers? If people mostly book online or walk in, a voice agent solves nothing. No calls, no product.

**The cost of a missed call test.** What is one customer worth? A missed call at a nail salon costs $60. A missed call at a personal injury law firm costs thousands. The higher that number, the easier every sales conversation gets and the more you can charge.

**The already spending test.** Do they already buy marketing, lead generation, or software? A business already paying $2,000 a month for Google Ads understands buying growth. A business that has never spent a dollar on marketing has to be convinced spending is normal before they can be convinced to spend on you.

A scored directory of specific niches is in [[12a - How To Read This Directory]], and the one day validation method is in [[12e - Validate A Niche In One Day]].

## Niches That Pass All Three

These are starting points, not the only options.

- Dental and orthodontic practices
- Med spas and aesthetics
- HVAC, plumbing, electrical
- Roofing and exterior contractors
- Law firms, especially personal injury and family law
- Auto repair and collision
- Veterinary clinics
- Real estate teams

What they share: phones ring, missed calls cost real money, and most already buy leads.

## Niches To Approach Carefully

Some verticals have regulatory weight that makes an AI answering the phone more complicated. Healthcare providers handling patient information, financial services, and anything touching legal advice all have rules about what can be recorded, stored, and disclosed.

That does not mean avoid them. Dental and med spa are on the good list above and are among the strongest verticals in this business. It means know the rules before you promise anything, and be honest with the client about what the agent will and will not do with their information. See [[03g - What The Agent Must Never Do]].

If you are going after dental or med spa, read [[10i - Healthcare And Regulated Niches]] before your first sales call. The two questions that come up are HIPAA (what counts as protected health information, and whether your agent and its transcripts touch it) and whether the client will ask you to sign a Business Associate Agreement, the BAA. Know your answer to both before the client asks, and confirm the details with a professional rather than guessing.

## Pick By What You Can Reach

The tiebreaker is access. If your uncle owns an HVAC company, start with HVAC. If you worked at a dental office, start there. Your first client is almost always someone who already trusts you, or someone in an industry whose language you already speak.

One niche. Ten clients in it. Then decide whether to add a second.
